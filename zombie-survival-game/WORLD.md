# A world the size of the real one

Owner's request (2026-11-03): copy real maps rather than hand-build them, make the world massive and feel like the real planet, use real assets, and give everything a lineage: a cause, and a history behind it.

## Where the real map comes from

| Data | What it gives | Licence | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **OpenStreetMap** | Every road, building outline, land use, river, shop, school, police station, power line and substation | ODbL: credit "© OpenStreetMap contributors"; a published database derived from it must stay ODbL | overpass-api.de (small areas), download.geofabrik.de (regions as `.osm.pbf`) |
| **Natural Earth** | Coastlines, countries, cities, rivers at planet scale | Public domain | npm `world-atlas` / `natural-earth` packages (reachable now) |
| **Elevation (DEM)** | Hills, valleys, coast heights | SRTM / Copernicus GLO-30: free with attribution | AWS open data terrain tiles |
| **Poly Haven / Kenney / ambientCG** | Textures, HDRIs, props and buildings | CC0 | api.polyhaven.com, kenney.nl, ambientcg.com |

**Blocked in this container today:** overpass-api.de, download.geofabrik.de, tile.openstreetmap.org, api.polyhaven.com and kenney.nl. Only the npm registry is reachable. To unlock real maps and assets, allow those hosts under the cloud environment's **Network access** setting (see https://code.claude.com/docs/en/cloud-environments#network-access).

## How a real place becomes playable

1. **Import offline, never at runtime.** A build script (`tools/world/`) reads an OSM extract and writes compact chunk files (roads, footprints, land use, points of interest). The game never calls OSM servers: it is fast, works offline, and respects their usage policy.
2. **Chunks of 256 m**, streamed around the player: terrain, roads and building shells load first, interiors on approach. Distant chunks are low-detail silhouettes. A city is thousands of chunks; a country is millions on disk, a handful in memory.
3. **Buildings from footprints.** OSM gives the outline, level count and use (`building=house`, `shop=supermarket`, `amenity=police`). Interiors are generated by rules for that use and size, seeded by the building's OSM id, so the same house always has the same rooms, and every building stays enterable (the VEIN promise, kept at planet scale).
4. **Infrastructure from the map itself.** OSM's power lines, substations, water towers and telecom masts feed the simulation: the grid fails along real lines, towns lose water when their own tower drains, and the police network sits in the real police station.
5. **Coordinates stay real.** Local metres per chunk, with each chunk's origin in latitude and longitude, so sun position, seasons and day length match the place.

## Lineage, the rule that ties it together

`src/sim/lineage.ts` is a causal graph from nature (fire, water, ice, ore, oil, sand, electric charge) through techniques and inventions to infrastructure and the items in your bag, with real dates. The ice trade leads to mechanical refrigeration, then the electric fridge and the cold chain, which is why the milk in a dead fridge spoils days after the grid fails. The telegraph leads to the transistor, Unix, TCP/IP and the police station's network.

`lineage.test.ts` fails the build if:

- anything has no cause, or an unknown cause
- there is a cycle
- something comes before its causes
- a node leads nowhere
- an item in the game has no lineage

Tap an item's name in the inventory to see its line, for example "Crude oil → Oil drilling and refining (1859) → Plastics (1907) → PET bottle (1973)".

**Next for lineage:**

## Order of work

1. The OSM-to-chunk importer, with a small real town as the first map. This needs Overpass or Geofabrik access.
2. Chunk streaming and building-shell generation from footprints.
3. Generated interiors by building use, reusing the current town's room and loot rules.
4. Infrastructure from map tags, wired into power, water and networks.
5. A planet-scale overview map from Natural Earth (already reachable) for travel between regions.
